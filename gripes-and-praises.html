A good can, paid for twice

A cleaner sidewalk should not make a perfectly usable purchase feel like money thrown away.

Submitted by a community member

Out with the old, in with the new

Opinion

You bought a garbage can. A good one. It has a secure lid, it holds the trash, and there is nothing wrong with it.

Now you need another one, not because the first can broke, but because it is not the official NYC Bin.

That is the part that deserves a gripe. Buying a can a week, a month or a year before the switch does not make that first purchase cost any less. The new requirement adds another bill while the old purchase still has plenty of life left.

For properties with 1-9 residential units, the official bin is now required for trash setout. Enforcement began September 8, 2026. A perfectly serviceable nonofficial trash can no longer meets that requirement.

That does not mean the old can must be thrown away. It may still have another use, including recycling where it meets the rules. But another use does not put the purchase price back in your pocket.

Cleaner streets and fewer rats are worth pursuing. The cost of getting there still matters. For a household watching every dollar, "just buy the new can" is not a small instruction.

A city asking residents to replace usable equipment should take that burden seriously. The policy may have a purpose. The second purchase still deserves an honest explanation.

Scope matters

This piece focuses on 1-9-unit properties, not every building in NYC. It does not claim mandatory disposal, a universal right to a refund, or a ticket for merely owning an older can.
